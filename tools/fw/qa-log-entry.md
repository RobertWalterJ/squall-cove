
## 2026-10-10 realistic baked fire wired in (branch fire-wiring, worktree squall-cove-fire; sw.js VERSION not bumped, set at merge)
Owner decision: the realistic baked fire is the direction. **How it is built so it merges.** All new code is ONE delimited region in index.html (`/*FW-REGION-START*/ ... /*FW-REGION-END*/`, right after the `window.__sc = {...}` line), built from `tools/fw/*.js` (order in `tools/fw/ORDER.txt`) by `python tools/fw/inject.py`. The edits to the existing game are one-line hooks listed in `tools/fw/hooklist.py` and applied (idempotent) by `python tools/fw/hooks.py`: the frame tick (`FW.tick(dt)` before `lightsTick`), the light budget (`FW.lights`), the old particle fires guarded by `!FW.drawn(owner)` (trees, wood objects, vehicles, wrecks, boat decks, burnArea, blast residue), blast() and debrisBurst(), waterSplash(), the fire-fighting monitor and truck, the bomber's water, lava cells and vents, geysers, the fixture builders (fixPlace, fixtureTick, layPointLights, lightsClear), battleStop and calm0 (FW.reset), the Settings button, a KEYMAP row, the audio manifest (`?v=1010`). `sw.js` has a comment only. `qa/voices-check.mjs` now accepts any `?v=N` on the manifest. Parity matrix: `#mFireQ` and a `fire-quality` capability.
**Part 1 fire.** Instanced camera-facing sprite renderer (`FW.R`: one InstancedBufferGeometry per atlas layer, 5 vec4 per instance, a few draw calls; straight alpha premultiplied in the shader; flame blends 55 percent additive; frame blending for the low-fps clips; near fade; the depth fade is a ground lift and a near fade, NOT a depth texture; 2 crossed quads for big fires; a 2-tap blur on the full-size smoke atlases and a little draw-time dither against banding). Atlases load lazily per type (full on desktop, `_low` on the phone and at Low) and are dropped after 90 s (25 s on the phone) without a fire of that type. Lifecycle machine: ignite, growth, burn loop (new variant and flip each loop), decay, out, extinguish by water (`FW.water`, rain at heavy intensity). Owners polled each frame: trees (crown, trunk or shrub from the tree kind), hot patches (burnArea), vehicles (engine, then whole vehicle, then burning wreck), boat decks, wooden objects; plus spot fires and spreading grass and brush FRONTS (tileable lead, body and trail rows per column, a smouldering strip behind, ash painted on the ground, licks, spot fires ahead in wind, speed from wind, dry soil, slope, rain), buildings (window, doorway, roof fires), lava lighting dry grass. Per-fire flickering warm point light through the existing budget (`light.flicker` and `stage_energy`) and a glow sprite for far fires; the baked haze descriptor of every type feeds `DIST.haze`; black smoke atlases for fuel, vehicle and boat fires plus a three-sprite soot column that widens and leans downwind; sensors: Thermal draws the heat atlases (magenta key colour, as the game's other hot things), Night vision blooms the flames, colour unchanged. Caps: desktop 40 animated fires (phone 14 at High, 8 at Low), 8 fronts (3 and 2 on the phone), 420 instances per layer; LOD by projected size (animated, static frame, culled). **Settings, Fire quality: High, Low, Off** (squall-cove-firequality; desktop High, phone Low; reduced motion and the smoothness governor lower High to Low; the governor then falls back to the old particles for 25 s). Fire sound (FIRE-SOUND-SPEC.md): loops by size class and fuel clustered within 15 m (+3 dB per doubling, capped), near and far crossfade, interior twin in the station, distant bed, one-shots (ember pop, spark, log crack, creak, glass, flare-ups by size, ignite, tank burst, lick, collapse, clatter, water hiss, out fade, sizzle) with the spec's gaps and voice caps; the old vehicle fire loop steps aside.
**Part 2 explosions.** blast() now plays the baked fireball (small, medium, ground, large, fuel for vehicle and fuel explosions; fireball, then plume, then residue, variant a or b), embers, sparks and ember streaks, with the shockwave and haze hooks unchanged; thrown chunks in the DEB pool carry flames (12 percent ordinary, 20 percent at R 8 or more, 60 to 70 percent vehicle and fuel), the carried-flame clip chosen by speed (4, 12, 30 m/s), stretched with speed, turned to the velocity, fading as it cools, embers and streaks behind; a landing chunk lights trees (igniteTreesNear), wooden objects, grass (spot fire) and buildings, makes a HEATP patch, and fizzles in water; thrown fuel (splash_fire, spatter fan) becomes pool and oil-slick fires with black smoke; water from boat monitors, the fire truck, the bomber and rain triggers the extinguish clip and a steam burst.
**Part 3 steam, mist, fog.** `assets/atmos` through the same renderer: steam burst on extinguish, steam_engine on damaged engines and cooling wrecks, steam_geyser on geysers, steam_lava where lava meets water, bow mist on fast boats, baked splashes (small, medium, large) replacing the procedural column, rain splashes on water, hose and monitor spray cone, and fog: world-anchored tiles of mist_dawn, fog_sea, fog_ground, fog_valley, fog_wisps, fog_forest, fog_bank and fog_blobs, three layers with their own heights and wind scroll, form, drift, thin and fall per tile; driven by the Sky Fog control (wx.fog), dawn (sun low, morning), valley fog on calm nights, and rain; sensors: Thermal draws the heat layer (steam bright) and fog faint and cool, Night vision washes fog out. Fog is capped (about 67 tiles on desktop, fewer on the phone) and respects Fire quality.
**Part 4 lights.** `assets/lights_pack.glb` is loaded once (the fixtures wait for it; the procedural models remain the fallback) and replaces the procedural tower, floodlight, lamp posts (cobra, harbour), generator and string lights; the lens swap goes through proxy lens objects (lamp_off to an emissive material, shot-out lamps dark, generator status lamp), and light positions, directions, cone and range come from the pack's `lightpt_*` empties; ground cables (tubes through sampled terrain heights) run from each generator socket to the lights it feeds; a sweeping searchlight (a spot light through the budget) stands at HQ, airfield and harbour points; strong-light effects: volumetric cone with the baked gradient and a hot-core copy (at most 6 on desktop, none on the phone), a beam-glow strip beyond about 100 m, a screen-sized lens flare (size by distance, bigger in haze, none from behind), a ground pool where the beam lands, a wet reflection smear in rain (the light textures carry their shape in alpha only, and the cone normal is built from the ring angle so the apex does not produce NaN); the haze factor from fog, mist, smoke and rain scales them. All gameplay is kept: generator blackout, shoot-out, capture power cut, bullet ray, explosions.
**Tests.** `node qa/fire-check.mjs desktop|phone` (own server on a free port, one headless Chrome killed by PID, software renderer): lazy loading per type, full against low atlases, lifecycle order and durations, extinguish clip, caps with 100 ignitions, haze feed, Thermal and Night vision uniforms and instance counts, a spread front advancing downwind, a burning chunk igniting a tree, a blast with flaming debris, fuel splash to pool fire, vehicle engine then full fire, boat deck fires and putting them out, steam burst on extinguish, baked splash, the Sky Fog control driving the fog, dawn and valley fog by time of day, a building fire, bow mist, hose cone and geyser, Fire quality Low and Off, sound loops and one-shots with voice limits (the audio context is started with autoplay allowed and muted), lights pack fixtures and cables, lit lamps and a shot-out lamp, flares, pools, cones and glow at night, texture unload, a staged performance run, phone budgets (draw calls at most 700, atlas memory under 60 MB), no console errors. `node qa/fire-shots.mjs` writes six pictures to `qa/shots/fire_*.png` (campfire close, grass front, vehicle fire with black smoke, explosion with flaming debris, fog bank over water at dawn, lit base at night with cones).
**Measured here (software renderer, so comparisons and not device figures).** Results: `qa/fire-check.mjs desktop` 37 of 37, `qa/fire-check.mjs phone` 37 of 37 (reports in qa/fire-check-report.json and qa/fire-check-phone-report.json). FW CPU per frame (FW.tick alone, 30 fires against none): desktop 0.35 ms to 0.51 ms mean (p95 0.6 to 0.9 ms); phone profile 0.20 ms to 0.46 ms (p95 0.6 to 1.3 ms). A whole software frame with 30 fires against none is within noise on desktop (about 4 s a frame for the night base scene either way) and about 20 percent slower on the phone profile (1.5 s to 1.9 s a frame); a real GPU was not available. Draw calls added by FW: about 18 to 29. The lights pack pieces are merged on load into one mesh per model (vertex colours from the pack's plain materials) plus one mesh per lens group, so the pack models cost 54 draw calls in the busiest lit-base view on desktop (1548 for the whole scene) and 32 on the phone profile (567 for the whole scene, budget 700); FW sprites add 15 to 29. Decoded atlases: about 7 MB per full-size type and 2.5 MB per phone type (34 types loaded in the test: 229 MB desktop; 22 types: 56 MB phone, the test loads far more types than a real game would), dropped when unused.
**Not verified.** Any real GPU (fill rate with a screen full of fire or fog, 40 fires on a phone, the cost of the extra spot light for the searchlight); real devices; anything by ear (the sound pack was checked by counts and voices, not heard); the Thermal and Night vision pictures (instance counts and uniforms only; the gunship station was not entered); bow mist and hose spray on moving boats seen in motion; the fog lifecycle over minutes; building fire and cable pictures; rain wet smears (no rain test picture); lava on water; the phone layout of the Fire quality button (not through measure-ui); the 25-minute parity guard (not run, as asked). Known limits: soft depth is a ground lift and near fade (no depth texture); the atlases show their baked dither when magnified; the first fireball of a type not yet loaded plays with the old particles; fire sound is clustered by size class and not by individual fire.
